# Interview Scoring Guide

## Overview

A consistent scoring rubric is essential for fair, calibrated interviews. This guide defines what each score level means across common evaluation dimensions, helping interviewers differentiate between strong and weak answers with clear criteria rather than subjective impression.

## Universal Score Scale (1-10)

**1-2 (Poor):** Candidate cannot address the question. Responses are incoherent, completely off-topic, or demonstrate fundamental misunderstanding. At this level, the candidate may refuse to answer, give a one-word response without elaboration, or describe an experience that contradicts the competency being assessed.

**3-4 (Below Average):** Candidate attempts an answer but lacks substance. Responses are vague, overly generic ("I'm a team player"), or describe activities without outcomes. The candidate may rely on hypotheticals ("I would do X") instead of real experience, or provide examples that are trivially simple for the role level.

**5-6 (Average):** Candidate provides a reasonable answer with some supporting evidence. Responses include a relevant example but may lack depth in one or more areas: limited quantification, unclear individual contribution, or surface-level reflection. This is the "tells you what happened but not why it mattered" zone.

**7-8 (Strong):** Candidate delivers a well-structured, evidence-based answer. Responses include specific context, clear individual actions, quantified results, and thoughtful reflection. The candidate demonstrates the competency convincingly and can handle follow-up probes without losing coherence. At 8, the answer reveals genuine insight or sophisticated judgment.

**9-10 (Exceptional):** Candidate provides a masterful answer that demonstrates deep expertise and mature judgment. At this level, responses show pattern recognition across situations, systemic thinking, and lessons that influenced broader organizational practice. Score 10 sparingly — it should represent answers that would impress the best practitioners in the field.

## Dimension-Specific Guidance

**Communication Clarity:**
- Score 3: Disorganized, hard to follow, excessive tangents
- Score 5: Understandable but lacks structure, some rambling
- Score 7: Well-organized with clear beginning-middle-end, concise
- Score 9: Exceptionally articulate, adapts explanation to audience level

**Problem-Solving Approach:**
- Score 3: No methodology, guesses at solutions, can't break down problems
- Score 5: Uses a basic framework but applies it rigidly, misses nuances
- Score 7: Demonstrates structured approach with hypothesis testing and iteration
- Score 9: Creates novel frameworks, identifies non-obvious constraints, elegant solutions

**Use of Examples:**
- Score 3: No concrete examples, only hypotheticals or generalities
- Score 5: Provides an example but it's vague or lacks key details
- Score 7: Specific example with context, actions, and quantified results
- Score 9: Rich, multi-layered example that reveals depth of experience and judgment

**Critical Thinking:**
- Score 3: Accepts premises uncritically, binary thinking, no tradeoff awareness
- Score 5: Identifies obvious tradeoffs but doesn't analyze them deeply
- Score 7: Articulates nuanced tradeoffs with evidence-based reasoning
- Score 9: Challenges assumptions, considers second-order effects, demonstrates intellectual humility

## Calibration Notes

Scores should reflect performance relative to the target role level. A strong answer from a junior candidate might merit a 7; the same answer from a senior candidate might merit a 5 because expectations are higher. Always anchor your scores to "what great looks like for this specific role at this specific level."
