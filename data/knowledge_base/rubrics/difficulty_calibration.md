# Difficulty Calibration Guide

## Overview

Interview difficulty should be calibrated to the candidate's target role and seniority level. Questions that are too easy fail to differentiate strong candidates; questions that are too hard create false negatives by testing knowledge beyond reasonable expectations. This guide defines what "easy," "medium," and "hard" mean across interview categories.

## Behavioral Questions

**Easy:** Questions about common workplace situations that any professional has encountered. These assess basic competencies and communication ability.
- "Tell me about a time you worked on a team."
- "Describe a project you're proud of."
- "How do you handle a tight deadline?"

Expected at: Intern, Junior, and early-career roles. Also useful as warm-up questions for any level.

**Medium:** Questions requiring specific, consequential examples with clear evidence of judgment and impact. These probe decision-making quality and self-awareness.
- "Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager's decision. How did you handle it?"
- "Describe a situation where you had to make a decision with incomplete information."
- "Walk me through a project that failed. What was your role in the failure?"

Expected at: Mid-level and senior individual contributors. The bar here is depth of reflection and quantified impact.

**Hard:** Questions that probe leadership, systemic thinking, and complex stakeholder dynamics. These require examples of influence at organizational scale.
- "Tell me about a time you changed an organization's approach to a problem, not just solved one instance."
- "Describe how you've handled a situation where doing the right thing for users conflicted with business objectives."
- "Walk me through your biggest leadership mistake and how it changed your management philosophy."

Expected at: Senior, Staff, and leadership roles. The bar is strategic thinking and organizational impact.

## Technical Questions

**Easy:** Fundamentals and core concepts. Tests whether the candidate has a working understanding of their domain.
- Explain the difference between a stack and a queue.
- What is normalization in databases?
- How does HTTP work at a high level?

**Medium:** Applied knowledge requiring synthesis across concepts. Tests whether the candidate can use their knowledge to solve real problems.
- Design a REST API for a task management system. What endpoints, status codes, and error handling would you use?
- Given a slow SQL query, walk me through how you'd diagnose and optimize it.
- How would you implement authentication and authorization in a web application?

**Hard:** Architecture and tradeoff-heavy questions with no single right answer. Tests depth of experience and judgment.
- Design a real-time notification system that handles 10 million users.
- How would you migrate a monolithic application to microservices without downtime?
- Compare eventual consistency vs. strong consistency for a financial transactions system.

## Case Questions

**Easy:** Structured problems with clear data and limited variables. Tests basic analytical reasoning.
- A coffee shop's revenue dropped 20% last quarter. What are the possible causes?
- Estimate the number of gas stations in your city.

**Medium:** Multi-variable problems requiring framework creation and quantitative analysis.
- Should Company X launch a new product line? Evaluate the opportunity.
- A ride-sharing company's driver retention is declining. Diagnose and recommend a solution.

**Hard:** Ambiguous problems with conflicting data, multiple stakeholders, and no clear right answer.
- A tech company is deciding between three acquisition targets. Evaluate each and recommend one.
- Design a pricing strategy for a two-sided marketplace entering a new geography.

## Difficulty Progression Strategies

**Gradual (default for junior roles):** Start with easy questions to build confidence, then ramp to medium. Only reach hard if the candidate is clearly exceeding expectations. This approach reduces false negatives from interview anxiety.

**Front-loaded (for senior roles):** Start with a hard question to immediately test the candidate's ceiling. If they struggle, step back to medium. This approach efficiently identifies whether the candidate meets the seniority bar.

**Adaptive:** Adjust dynamically based on evaluator signals. Increase difficulty after strong answers, decrease after weak ones. This approach finds the candidate's true level most efficiently but requires real-time evaluation feedback.
