# Hiring Decision Levels

## Overview

This rubric maps interview performance to a standardized hiring recommendation scale. Each level represents a distinct signal that calibrates expectations across interviewers and helps translate subjective impressions into consistent decisions.

## Decision Scale

### Strong Hire (Score 8.0-10.0)

The candidate consistently exceeded expectations across most evaluation dimensions. They demonstrated deep expertise, sophisticated judgment, and strong communication. Their answers revealed not just competence but genuine insight — the kind of thinking that elevates the team.

**Indicators:**
- Provided multiple strong examples with quantified impact
- Demonstrated strategic thinking beyond the immediate question
- Showed exceptional self-awareness and growth trajectory
- Handled difficult follow-up questions with poise and depth
- Would be a clear asset to the team from day one

**Interviewer confidence:** High conviction this candidate would succeed and raise the bar.

### Hire (Score 6.5-7.9)

The candidate met expectations with several moments of strength. They showed solid competence in core areas, communicated effectively, and provided evidence-based answers. There may be areas for growth, but the overall signal is positive.

**Indicators:**
- Provided relevant examples with reasonable specificity
- Showed good problem-solving methodology
- Communicated clearly with adequate structure
- Demonstrated awareness of tradeoffs and limitations
- Would perform well in the role with standard onboarding

**Interviewer confidence:** Comfortable this candidate would succeed in the role.

### Lean Hire (Score 5.5-6.4)

The candidate showed potential but with notable gaps. Some answers were strong while others lacked depth or specificity. The overall signal is cautiously positive — the candidate could succeed but carries more risk than a clear Hire.

**Indicators:**
- Mixed quality across answers — some strong, some weak
- Showed competence in core areas but lacked depth in others
- Communication was adequate but could be more structured
- Examples were relevant but occasionally vague
- Would need additional support or mentoring to fully succeed

**Interviewer confidence:** Tentatively positive, but would want to discuss with the hiring panel.

### Lean No Hire (Score 4.0-5.4)

The candidate showed some relevant skills but significant gaps that raise concerns about their ability to perform in the role. Answers lacked depth, specificity, or evidence of the required competencies.

**Indicators:**
- Struggled to provide concrete examples for key competencies
- Problem-solving approach was superficial or disorganized
- Communication lacked clarity or structure
- Limited evidence of the self-awareness expected for the role level
- Would require substantial investment to bring up to expectations

**Interviewer confidence:** Concerns outweigh positives. Would not advocate for this candidate.

### No Hire (Score 1.0-3.9)

The candidate did not demonstrate the competencies required for the role. Answers were consistently weak, off-topic, or revealed fundamental misunderstandings. This is a clear signal that the candidate is not ready for this position.

**Indicators:**
- Unable to provide relevant examples or used only hypotheticals
- Demonstrated poor judgment or reasoning in key areas
- Communication was disorganized or difficult to follow
- Showed no awareness of gaps or areas for growth
- Would not succeed in the role even with significant support

**Interviewer confidence:** Strong conviction this is not the right candidate for this role.

## Using This Scale

Interviewers should assign a rating based on the overall pattern of performance, not a single strong or weak answer. Weight dimensions according to the role-specific rubric — a Hire-level score in a heavily weighted dimension matters more than an exceptional score in a lightly weighted one.
