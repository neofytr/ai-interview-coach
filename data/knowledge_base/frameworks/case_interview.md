# Case Interview Framework

## Overview

Case interviews test a candidate's ability to structure ambiguous problems, analyze data, and synthesize recommendations under pressure. They are standard in consulting, product management, and strategy roles but increasingly appear in tech interviews for analytical positions.

## Core Competencies Assessed

**Problem Structuring:** Can the candidate break an ambiguous question into manageable components? Strong candidates create MECE (Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive) frameworks on the fly rather than forcing pre-memorized structures. The best signal is when a candidate pauses, asks clarifying questions, then lays out a logical structure before diving in.

**Quantitative Reasoning:** Can the candidate work with numbers comfortably? This includes market sizing (Fermi estimation), back-of-envelope calculations, and interpreting data tables. Candidates should show their work, state assumptions explicitly, and sanity-check answers against intuition.

**Hypothesis-Driven Thinking:** Does the candidate form hypotheses and test them, or do they meander through analysis without direction? Strong candidates say "I think the issue is X because of Y — let me check by looking at Z" rather than asking for every available data point.

**Synthesis and Recommendation:** Can the candidate distill analysis into a clear, actionable recommendation? The best answers follow the pyramid principle: lead with the answer, then support with 2-3 key reasons, each backed by evidence.

## Evaluation Signals

**Strong candidate indicators:**
- Asks clarifying questions before structuring (shows they don't jump to conclusions)
- Creates a custom framework rather than applying a generic one
- Makes explicit assumptions and revisits them when new information arrives
- Quantifies impact when recommending a path forward
- Acknowledges risks and proposes mitigations

**Weak candidate indicators:**
- Applies memorized frameworks without adapting to the specific problem
- Performs calculations without explaining assumptions
- Provides analysis without a clear recommendation
- Ignores implementation feasibility
- Cannot adjust when initial assumptions prove wrong

## Types of Case Questions

**Market entry:** Should company X enter market Y? Assess market size, competitive landscape, capabilities, and financial viability.

**Profitability:** Revenue is declining — diagnose and recommend. Decompose into revenue (price × volume) and cost (fixed + variable) trees.

**Estimation:** How many piano tuners are in Chicago? Test structured decomposition and comfort with uncertainty.

**Operations:** How would you reduce delivery times by 30%? Test process thinking and constraint identification.

## Interviewer Adaptation

Increase difficulty by adding constraints, introducing contradictory data, or asking candidates to pivot their recommendation. Decrease difficulty by offering structural hints or simplifying the data. The goal is to find the candidate's ceiling, not to trick them.
