# Competency-Based Interview Framework

## Overview

Competency-based interviewing evaluates candidates against predefined behavioral competencies required for success in a role. Unlike unstructured interviews that rely on gut feeling, this approach uses consistent criteria across all candidates, improving fairness and predictive validity.

## Core Competencies by Category

**Leadership and Influence:**
- Setting direction and inspiring others to follow
- Making decisions with incomplete information
- Influencing without authority across organizational boundaries
- Building and developing high-performing teams

Evaluation focus: Look for evidence of initiative, ownership of outcomes (not just activities), and the ability to articulate how they motivated others. Senior candidates should show they built systems and processes, not just executed tasks.

**Problem Solving and Analytical Thinking:**
- Decomposing complex problems into actionable components
- Using data to inform decisions rather than relying on intuition alone
- Identifying root causes versus symptoms
- Adapting approach when initial solutions fail

Evaluation focus: Probe for the reasoning process, not just the outcome. Ask "walk me through how you decided" rather than "what did you do." Strong candidates show iterative thinking — they form hypotheses, test them, and adjust.

**Communication and Collaboration:**
- Translating technical concepts for non-technical stakeholders
- Active listening and incorporating diverse perspectives
- Managing conflict constructively
- Writing clearly and concisely

Evaluation focus: The interview itself IS the test. Observe how the candidate structures their responses, handles follow-up questions, and adapts their communication style to the conversation.

**Execution and Delivery:**
- Planning and prioritizing under constraints
- Managing ambiguity and changing requirements
- Balancing speed with quality
- Measuring and communicating progress

Evaluation focus: Look for concrete examples with timelines, scope decisions, and measurable outcomes. Candidates who only describe what they built without explaining how they managed the process reveal a gap in execution maturity.

## Scoring Rubric

Each competency should be scored on a consistent scale:

- **1-3 (Below expectations):** No evidence or negative evidence. Candidate cannot provide relevant examples or provides examples that demonstrate poor judgment.
- **4-5 (Meets expectations):** Adequate evidence with some depth. Candidate provides relevant examples but may lack specificity or quantified impact.
- **6-7 (Exceeds expectations):** Strong evidence with clear impact. Candidate demonstrates sophisticated thinking, quantifies results, and shows learning.
- **8-10 (Exceptional):** Outstanding evidence with systemic impact. Candidate shows pattern recognition across situations, built repeatable processes, and influenced beyond their immediate scope.

## Interviewer Best Practices

Ask open-ended questions that start with "Tell me about a time when..." followed by targeted probes. Avoid hypothetical questions ("What would you do if...") — past behavior predicts future behavior more reliably than stated intentions. Use a consistent rubric across all candidates for the same role to reduce bias.

## Adapting to Seniority

Weight competencies differently by level. For junior roles, emphasize learning agility, coachability, and foundational skills. For senior roles, emphasize strategic thinking, cross-functional influence, and organizational impact. For leadership roles, emphasize talent development, vision setting, and navigating ambiguity at scale.
