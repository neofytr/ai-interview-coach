# STAR Method — Behavioral Interview Framework

## Overview

The STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the gold standard for structuring behavioral interview responses. It forces candidates to move beyond vague claims and provide concrete, verifiable evidence of their competencies.

## Components

**Situation:** Set the context. Where were you working? What was the team size? What was the business context? Strong candidates provide enough detail to make the scenario real without over-explaining. The situation should be specific — "at my last company" is weak; "on the payments team at a Series B fintech serving 50K merchants" is strong.

**Task:** Define your specific responsibility. What was expected of you? What was the challenge? This separates individual contribution from team effort. Interviewers want to know what YOU owned, not what the team did. Candidates who blur this distinction often get marked down on ownership and accountability.

**Action:** Describe exactly what you did. This is the most critical component and should consume 50-60% of the answer. Strong candidates walk through their decision-making process: what options they considered, why they chose their approach, what tradeoffs they accepted. Weak candidates describe outcomes without explaining the reasoning that led there.

**Result:** Quantify the impact. Revenue increased by X%, deployment time decreased from Y to Z, customer complaints dropped by N%. Results without numbers feel unsubstantiated. If exact metrics aren't available, use directional indicators: "reduced by roughly half" beats "it got better."

## Evaluation Criteria

When assessing STAR responses, interviewers evaluate:

- **Specificity:** Does the candidate name real projects, tools, and metrics, or do they speak in generalities?
- **Ownership:** Does the candidate clearly articulate their individual contribution versus the team's?
- **Decision reasoning:** Does the candidate explain WHY they chose their approach, not just WHAT they did?
- **Impact quantification:** Are results tied to measurable business outcomes?
- **Proportionality:** Is the example appropriately scoped for the level of the role?

## Common Pitfalls

Candidates frequently under-invest in the Action component, rushing to the Result. They also tend to choose safe examples that don't reveal much — the best STAR responses involve genuine difficulty, real stakes, and honest reflection on what they would do differently. Interviewers should probe candidates who give overly polished answers by asking "what would you change if you could do it again?"

## Adapting by Role Level

Junior candidates should focus on learning agility and coachability in their STAR stories. Senior candidates should demonstrate strategic thinking and cross-functional influence. Leadership candidates should show how they built systems and developed others, not just delivered results themselves.
